## 1 Software and independence

The Provider gives the Operator nonexclusive, nontransferable access to the hosted TEMBY platform for its lawful purchasing business. The Provider retains its software rights; no source code or ownership is transferred. Third-party components remain subject to their licences. The Provider may serve other operators. Neither party is the other's employee, partner or general agent, and neither may bind the other beyond the technical permission below.

The Provider manages and pays for the hosting and infrastructure unless otherwise agreed in writing. It must use reasonable care in technical delivery, protect confidential data, restrict access between operators, and meet its own applicable legal duties.

## 2 The Operator runs purchasing

The Operator independently supplies its purchasing service. It is responsible for lawful currency sourcing and funding, payment permissions, customer purchasing terms, approved pricing, merchant purchases, applicable import formalities, fulfillment, customer support, refunds, taxes and required transaction records. It must supply evidence of its registration, payment permissions and actual funding/purchasing method before live use, keep that information accurate, and promptly disclose relevant restrictions or changes.

Customer purchase payments go directly to the Operator's approved merchant account. The Provider does not hold those funds, supply or exchange currency, place purchases or undertake delivery or refunds. The platform may calculate quotes, record orders and initiate payment requests under the Operator's approved rules and payment arrangement. This permission gives no other commercial authority.

The website, checkout and advertisements must clearly identify the Operator as the purchasing and fulfillment business, with its contact details. The Operator must disclose fees and obtain customer agreement before extra charges or material changes to paid orders. Neither party may misrepresent merchant affiliation, permissions or the other's commitments. Actual conduct must match these responsibilities.

No settlement may admit the Provider's fault or impose obligations on it without its written consent.
